# 🇭🇰 Hong Kong (香港)

> **Why visit:** Victoria Harbour and its iconic skyline, the Star Ferry, Victoria Peak, traditional street markets and cha chaan teng cafés, outlying islands, and the Tian Tan Buddha on Lantau Island. This guide organizes transportation, sights, payment methods, and money-saving tips so visitors can put together their own routes.

## 📍 Overview

- **Main areas:** Tsim Sha Tsui, Mong Kok, and Yau Ma Tei in Kowloon; Central, Admiralty, Causeway Bay, and Victoria Peak on Hong Kong Island; Tung Chung, Ngong Ping, Tian Tan Buddha, and Tai O on Lantau Island.
- **Weather:** Summers are hot and humid. Alternate outdoor waterfront walks with air-conditioned museums and shopping malls. Autumn and winter are better for hiking, harbor views, and exploring neighborhoods.
- **Transportation:** MTR, buses, the Star Ferry, and other ferries. Airport options include the Airport Express, A/E airport buses, and the cheaper Tung Chung Line plus S1 bus combination.
- **Budget-friendly ideas:** Cross the harbor by Star Ferry, change to a bus at Sheung Shui, eat at local markets and neighborhood restaurants in Yau Ma Tei and Mong Kok, and travel to or from the airport via Tung Chung and the S1 bus.

# 🚆 Getting to Hong Kong from Shenzhen: Land and Sea

## 🚇 Land Route 1: Luohu Border Crossing → MTR East Rail Line

1. Take **Shenzhen Metro Line 1** to its terminus, **Luohu Station (罗湖站)**.
2. Walk to the **Luohu border checkpoint** and clear immigration, then proceed to **Lo Wu Station (罗湖站)** on the Hong Kong side.
3. Take the **MTR East Rail Line** south toward Kowloon and Hong Kong Island, changing trains as needed.
4. For Tsim Sha Tsui, take the MTR toward **East Tsim Sha Tsui**. The original notes give an ordinary-class fare of about **HK$42** and a journey of roughly **56 minutes** from Lo Wu.

## 🚇 Land Route 2: Futian Border Crossing → Lok Ma Chau → Sheung Shui

1. Take **Shenzhen Metro Line 4** to **Futian Checkpoint Station (福田口岸)** and clear immigration.
2. On the Hong Kong side, enter **Lok Ma Chau Station (落马洲站)** and take the East Rail Line **one stop** to **Sheung Shui (上水)**.
3. Continue by MTR or switch to a bus bound for Kowloon.

### 💰 Save Money: Change to a Bus at Sheung Shui

- At **Sheung Shui Bus Terminus**, take **KMB route 270A** toward Kowloon/Tsim Sha Tsui. The reference fare in the notes is **HK$16.2**.
- Other routes mentioned include **276B**; choose according to the destination.
- If most travel that day will be by bus, there is no need to buy an MTR Tourist Day Pass. Some MTR interchanges also involve fairly long walks.

## ⛴️ By Sea: Ferry from Shenzhen to Hong Kong

- Ferry travel is an option for visitors departing from Shenzhen passenger terminals, including those connecting onward to Hong Kong International Airport.
- **Ferries entering Hong Kong for a city visit** and **airport SkyPier transit services** are different products. Choose the route based on whether you intend to enter Hong Kong and whether you need to check baggage.
- For a flight departing from Hong Kong, compare ferries with the Luohu/Futian land crossings in terms of baggage handling, journey time, and cost.

## 🛂 Transiting Hong Kong en Route to Mainland China or a Third Destination

- Chinese passport holders traveling via Hong Kong to mainland China or a third destination can explain to the airline that **Hong Kong is a transit point**, and have onward flight or ferry tickets ready. The notes discuss transit arrangements that do not require a Hong Kong entry permit.
- Airport transit may not require entering Hong Kong if travelers already have the onward boarding pass and bags are checked through. If bags need to be collected and checked in again, follow the relevant transit and immigration procedures.
- References: [Hong Kong Immigration Department: Entry Arrangements for Chinese Passport Holders](https://www.immd.gov.hk/eng/services/visas/overseas-chinese-entry-arrangement.html#21), [Hong Kong Airport Transfer/Transit Guide](https://www.hongkongairport.com/tc/passenger-guide/transfer-transit/#2).

# 💳 Paying for Transportation and Choosing Passes

## 🚇 MTR

- **Octopus** is widely used for MTR and bus rides as well as small purchases at many merchants.
- **Alipay and WeChat transit QR codes** can be used on the MTR heavy-rail network, excluding Airport Express stations.
- Payment options for MTR tickets and tourist passes—cash, digital wallets, or credit cards—differ by ticket machine, station, and product. Keeping some Hong Kong dollars is useful at border stations.
- At Lo Wu and Lok Ma Chau, Octopus top-up machines and some cross-border transit cards may require assistance at a staffed counter for an initial top-up.

## 🎫 MTR Tourist Day Pass

- Useful for visitors making many MTR journeys in a day; validity is **24 hours from the first entry through the fare gates**.
- The source notes list historical adult prices of **HK$65/HK$75** and a child price of **HK$30**. The Child Tourist Day Pass is for ages **3–11**.
- Some border stations do not sell these passes; the notes suggest buying one at a city station such as Sheung Shui.
- **Is it worth buying?** Perhaps not if you only need one or two MTR trips and plan to use buses or walk the rest of the day.
- [MTR Tourist Day Pass](https://www.mtr.com.hk/ch/customer/tickets/day_pass_tourist.html)

## 🚌 Buses: Contactless Cards, Apple Pay, Alipay, and WeChat

- **Visa, Mastercard, and Apple Pay contactless payment** are supported on KMB, Long Win Bus, and Citybus services as described in the notes.
- Alipay and WeChat bus payment options are also mentioned for KMB and Citybus. Since the payment experience can differ by route, carry cash as a backup if a WeChat payment fails.
- **Buses generally do not give change**, so have the correct fare if paying cash.
- Child and senior discounts vary by route and ticket type. The notes also mention concessions for children under 12 and seniors, as well as accompanied children under three who do not occupy a seat.

## 🎟️ Which Transport Option Fits Best?

| Ticket / Option | Best For | Notes |

# ✈️ Hong Kong Airport: Cheap Transportation, Baggage, and Departure Tax

## 🚌 From the City to the Airport: Three Common Options

### 💰 Option 1: Tung Chung Line → S1 (Budget Route)

1. Take the MTR **Tung Chung Line** to its terminus, **Tung Chung Station (东涌站)**.
2. Exit and walk to the nearby **S1 airport shuttle bus stop**.
3. Ride the S1 to the airport terminal. The original notes give a fare of around **HK$3.7** and a ride of a little over ten minutes.

**Luggage tip:** Transfers to the Tung Chung Line around Central can involve long walks. Travelers with heavy suitcases may prefer an airport bus.

### 🧳 Option 2: Airport A Routes (Convenient with Luggage)

- A-prefixed airport buses run from various city neighborhoods to the airport. For example, **A11 from around Admiralty** has a reference fare of **HK$41.9**.
- The bus stops at the **departures level**, near luggage carts, making it convenient with suitcases.
- [A11 Route Reference](https://hkbus.fandom.com/wiki/%E5%9F%8E%E5%B7%B4A11%E7%B7%9A)

### 🚌 Option 3: Airport E Routes

- E-prefixed buses also connect the airport with city districts and the Tung Chung area, offering another budget-friendly choice.
- They may make more stops than express-style airport transport. Find an A or E route serving your area using the airport bus directory.
- [Hong Kong International Airport: Public Buses](https://www.hongkongairport.com/tc/transport/to-from-airport/public-buses.page#content2)

**Another choice: Airport Express.** Faster but more expensive; reaching its platforms from some Central-area connections can involve considerable walking.

## 🧳 Early Check-in and Baggage Drop at the Airport

- Check-in can be available well ahead of departure at Hong Kong International Airport. The notes mention that **Cathay Pacific allows check-in and baggage drop about 24 hours in advance**.
- One approach is to arrive at the airport in the morning, check in and drop bags at Cathay Pacific, then take the **S1 bus** to Tung Chung without luggage.
- **A 24-hour airport does not mean that every airline accepts bags around the clock.** Ask the airline counter about its earliest check-in time.
- [Cathay Pacific Check-in and Boarding Pass FAQ](https://www.cathaypacific.com/cx/zh_HK/faqs/check-in/check-in-kiosk/what-are-the-earliest-and-latest-times-that-I-can-check-in-and-collect-my-boarding-pass.html)

## 🧾 Air Passenger Departure Tax Exemption and Refund

- Hong Kong's Air Passenger Departure Tax increased from **HK$120** to **HK$200**. The exemptions described in the notes include eligible passengers who **arrive by air and depart by air on the same or following day**, and those who **enter Hong Kong by a non-air route and depart from the airport on the same or following day**.
- This is particularly relevant for eligible travelers **arriving from Shenzhen by land or sea and then flying out of Hong Kong**.

### 📋 Practical Refund Steps

1. Keep **all boarding passes**, and photograph them as backups.
2. Ask the airline for an **e-ticket receipt or invoice with an itemized tax breakdown**, especially if the ticket was issued by a different airline from the operating carrier.
3. Apply through the [APDT Refund Portal](https://www.apdtrefund.hk/).
4. The notes also describe submitting the application by phone after departure formalities, receiving a QR code, and collecting Hong Kong dollar cash from a designated airport currency-exchange counter.
5. Alipay, WeChat, and bank card refunds are also discussed, but fees may apply. **Collecting cash at the airport** is an option worth comparing.

**Application period:** The notes indicate that eligible passengers, including children and transit passengers, may apply within **28 days after the flight departs**.

### 🏨 Accommodation and Shopping Near Tung Chung

- **Novotel Citygate Hong Kong:** The notes mention an airport shuttle, making the hotel convenient for shopping in Tung Chung or visiting Lantau Island.
- **Citygate Outlets:** An outlet mall next to Tung Chung Station; the notes also mention a supermarket on a lower level.
- Luggage storage references: [Citygate Mall Services](https://www.citygateoutlets.com.hk/zh-hk/visit/mall-services/), [Ngong Ping 360 Guest Services](https://www.np360.com.hk/tc/visitor-information/tourist-guide#guest-services-section).

# 🌃 Tsim Sha Tsui, Victoria Harbour, and the Star Ferry

## 🌊 Avenue of Stars and Harbour Views

- **Avenue of Stars (星光大道):** Enjoy the Hong Kong Island skyline and waterfront, including the Bruce Lee statue. Good for photos both day and night.
- **A Symphony of Lights (幻彩咏香江):** Watch the harbor light show around **8:00 p.m.**
- **Hong Kong Museum of Art (香港艺术馆):** A great break during a Tsim Sha Tsui waterfront walk. The museum has **air conditioning and places to sit**, and its harbor-facing areas offer views of Victoria Harbour and the Hong Kong Island skyline while you rest.
- Other viewpoints include the **Hung Hom and Kwun Tong promenades** and **Lei Yue Mun**, offering different skyline and sunset perspectives.

## ⛴️ Star Ferry: Tsim Sha Tsui → Central

1. After walking along the Avenue of Stars, head to the **Tsim Sha Tsui Star Ferry Pier**.
2. Follow signs for the **Central** service, board, and cross Victoria Harbour.
3. Enjoy views of skyscrapers on both the Kowloon and Hong Kong Island shores from the water.
4. Arrive at the **Central Star Ferry Pier**. Nearby attractions include the **Hong Kong Observation Wheel** and **Hong Kong Maritime Museum**; you can also catch a bus onward to Victoria Peak.

- The **regular commuter ferry** and **Victoria Harbour sightseeing cruises** are separate services. Sightseeing cruise ticket counters accept payment options described in the notes, including cash, credit cards, Alipay, and WeChat Pay.
- [Star Ferry Routes and Services](https://www.starferry.com.hk/tc/service)

## 🖼️ More Museums Around Tsim Sha Tsui

- **Hong Kong Space Museum:** In the Tsim Sha Tsui cultural district, easy to combine with a waterfront walk.
- **Hong Kong Science Museum:** Popular with families. The original notes mention walking through the Tsim Sha Tsui underground passage toward **P2**, where there is a lift.
- **Free admission:** The notes highlight **free admission on Wednesdays** to the Science Museum and Space Museum.

# ⛰️ Victoria Peak: Bus and Walking Routes

## 🚌 Citybus 15 / X15 from Central

**Suggested combination:** Tsim Sha Tsui → Star Ferry → Central Piers → Victoria Peak.

1. After arriving at the **Central Star Ferry Pier**, head toward **Central Pier 5 / nearby bus stops**. Other boarding points noted include **Central Exchange Square** and around **Admiralty Exit C1**.
2. Take **Citybus 15 or X15** toward **The Peak**.
3. Walk to the **Lion's Pavilion (狮子亭)** for views over Victoria Harbour, Hong Kong Island's towers, and the Kowloon skyline.
4. Late afternoon and evening are particularly popular for skyline views.

## 🚶 Walking Uphill from Central

- An alternative is to use the **Central–Mid-Levels Escalator** to climb partway through the city, then follow walking directions toward the Peak. The notes mention approximately **40 minutes of walking** after the escalator section.
- To avoid a long climb, choose bus 15/X15 instead.

# 🪷 Lantau Island: Tung Chung, Ngong Ping, Tian Tan Buddha, and Tai O

## 🚌 Bus 23 from Tung Chung to Tian Tan Buddha

1. Travel to **Tung Chung MTR Station**. From the airport, the **S1** reaches the Tung Chung area in a little over ten minutes.
2. Follow signs across the road toward **Ngong Ping / Lantau buses** and find the **New Lantao Bus route 23** stop.
3. Take **route 23** to Ngong Ping to visit the **Tian Tan Buddha (天坛大佛)** and **Po Lin Monastery (宝莲禅寺)**.
4. Continue on foot to the **Wisdom Path (心经简林)**, choosing an easy stroll or additional hiking according to your energy level.

- The notes mention a **failed WeChat payment on route 23**; carry cash or another payment option.
- **ATMs can be hard to find around Ngong Ping Village**, so bring Hong Kong dollars from Tung Chung.

## 🚠 Ngong Ping 360 Cable Car

- The **Ngong Ping 360** cable car is another way to travel from Tung Chung to Ngong Ping, with views of Lantau's mountains and coast.
- After arriving, the sightseeing sequence is the same: **Tian Tan Buddha → Po Lin Monastery → Wisdom Path**.

## 🛕 Suggested Walking Order at Ngong Ping

**Tian Tan Buddha → Po Lin Monastery (lunch is possible) → Wisdom Path → Ngong Ping Village → return to Tung Chung or continue to Tai O.**

- **Po Lin Monastery:** Visit the temple and consider a vegetarian lunch.
- **Tian Tan Buddha:** Suitable for visitors of different ages, with additional hiking options nearby.
- **Lantau Peak (凤凰山):** A more demanding mountain hike suited to fit, experienced hikers.

## 🛶 From Ngong Ping to Tai O, Then by Boat

- After exploring Ngong Ping, take a **bus to Tai O (大澳)** to see the fishing village and waterfront streets.
- It may also be possible to leave Tai O by water rather than retracing the entire bus journey; choose a ferry based on your destination.
- [Tai O Ferry Information](https://www.fortuneferry.com.hk/zh/news/sailing-news-tuen-mun-to-tai-o-route/36)

# 🏙️ More Hong Kong Attractions and Neighborhoods

## 🎢 Ocean Park and Disneyland

- **Ocean Park Hong Kong:** Worth a full day for visitors who enjoy animals, marine themes, and amusement rides.
- **Hong Kong Disneyland:** Popular with families and theme park fans. Ticket references: [Klook](https://www.klook.com/zh-TW/activity/39-hong-kong-disneyland-resort-hong-kong/), [KKday](https://www.kkday.com/zh-my/product/2301-hong-kong-disneyland-ticket).

## 🌿 Hong Kong Island and Cultural Attractions

- **Repulse Bay and Stanley:** Beaches, seaside walks, and scenery on the south side of Hong Kong Island.
- **Wong Tai Sin Temple:** A religious and cultural attraction.
- **Hong Kong Observation Wheel:** By the Central waterfront, easy to visit after the Star Ferry.
- **Hong Kong Maritime Museum:** Near the Central waterfront, covering the development of Hong Kong from fishing port to international harbor. Air-conditioned and convenient in hot weather.
- **Hong Kong Palace Museum:** For art and historical artifacts, and a possible addition to a walk around the West Kowloon Cultural District and waterfront.

## 🛍️ Shopping Districts, Markets, and Everyday Purchases

- **Mong Kok and Yau Ma Tei:** Neighborhoods with more everyday local shopping, traditional markets, small stores, and dai pai dong-style eateries. Good for budget-friendly meals and household goods.
- **Yau Ma Tei dai pai dong-style eateries:** An option for inexpensive local food away from purely tourist-oriented restaurants.
- **Causeway Bay:** A busy shopping and dining district, home to **Times Square** and **SOGO**.
- **The Landmark, Central:** Upscale brands and boutiques.
- **Pacific Place, Admiralty:** High-end shopping, convenient to other Hong Kong Island attractions.
- **Citygate Outlets, Tung Chung:** Convenient en route to the airport or Lantau; the notes mention a supermarket downstairs.

# 🍜 Hong Kong Food and Dining Customs

## 🥢 Foods to Try

- **Hong Kong-style dim sum / yum cha:** Tea and dim sum, often best from around **9:00 a.m. onward**.
- **Cantonese dishes and slow-cooked soups:** Traditional Cantonese food, nourishing soups, and a wide variety of dim sum.
- **Seafood:** Oysters, fish, and shrimp are especially good for sharing in a group.
- **Desserts:** Double-skin milk and mango-coconut sweets; the notes mention **Hui Lau Shan (许留山)** mango desserts.
- **Local dai pai dong-style restaurants:** Yau Ma Tei offers options for inexpensive neighborhood dining.
- **Well-known restaurant options:** Cantonese restaurants such as **Yung Kee (镛记)** and **Lei Garden (利苑)**, depending on budget and preference.

## 🕰️ Meal Times

- Dim sum can be planned for around **9:00–11:00 a.m.**
- Lunch may be as late as **2:00 p.m.**, and dinner often falls around **7:00–8:00 p.m.**
- Eating outside the busiest meal periods can reduce waiting time.

# 🧭 Transportation and Budget Travel Tips

- **Tsim Sha Tsui to Central:** Take the **Star Ferry** for both transport and Victoria Harbour scenery.
- **Central to Victoria Peak:** Take **bus 15/X15**, then walk to the Lion's Pavilion.
- **Airport to Tung Chung:** Take the **S1 bus**; transfer at Tung Chung to **bus 23** for Ngong Ping and the Big Buddha.
- **Shenzhen to Kowloon:** Cross by land, then transfer at **Sheung Shui** to a Kowloon-bound bus for a cheaper trip.
- **Leaving from Hong Kong Airport:** Compare the **Tung Chung Line + S1**, **A/E airport buses**, and **Airport Express** for cost and luggage convenience.
- **Payment:** Carry an **Octopus card, contactless bank card / Apple Pay, and some Hong Kong dollar change**. Alipay and WeChat are useful additional options.
- **Taxis:** Keep cash on hand; **Uber** is convenient in Hong Kong.
- **Hot weather:** Include stops at the **Hong Kong Museum of Art, Maritime Museum, Science Museum, or Space Museum** for air conditioning and rest.
- **Free admission days:** The notes highlight **Wednesdays at the Hong Kong Science Museum and Space Museum**.
