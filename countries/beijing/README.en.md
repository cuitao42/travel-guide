# 🇨🇳 Beijing (北京)

> **Why visit Beijing?** Beijing brings together imperial palaces and gardens, the monumental Central Axis, the Great Wall, historic hutongs and temples, science museums, universities, and modern Olympic landmarks. This guide groups attractions, transportation, reservations, and food by area, with flexible sightseeing routes that visitors can mix and match.

## 📍 Overview

- **Suggested stay:** Allow around **5–7 days** for a first visit. Stay longer if you especially enjoy history and museums or want to see both sections of the Great Wall.
- **Major sights:** Tiananmen Square, the Forbidden City, Jingshan Park, the National Museum of China, the Summer Palace, the Temple of Heaven, the Great Wall, Yonghe Temple, the Imperial College, and the China Science and Technology Museum.
- **Best seasons:** Spring and autumn are ideal for long walks. **Summers are hot and sunny**, particularly in palace courtyards and on the Great Wall; bring water and sun protection. Winters are cold and dry, with strong winds along exposed sections of the Wall.
- **Getting around:** Use the subway, buses, and walking in the city. For the Great Wall, consider high-speed rail, tourist buses, organized shuttles, or a private car.
- **Where to stay:** Wangfujing, Qianmen/Dashilan, Chongwenmen, and Dongdan are convenient for central attractions and subway connections.

# 🏯 The Forbidden City, Tiananmen, and Beijing's Central Axis

## 🇨🇳 Tiananmen Square: Flag Ceremonies and Nearby Landmarks

- **Flag raising and lowering:** Choose the early-morning flag-raising ceremony or the evening flag-lowering ceremony. Raising times vary with sunrise. Arrive early to allow for lines, security screening, and walking; reserve your visit in advance.
- **Tiananmen Square:** Combine the square with the Monument to the People's Heroes, views of the Great Hall of the People, and the National Museum of China. Reserve entry in advance.
- **Chairman Mao Memorial Hall:** Located on the south side of the square. Take note of reservation procedures and restrictions on personal belongings.
- **Tiananmen Rostrum:** Visitors interested in going up onto the historic gate tower can combine it with sightseeing near the Forbidden City entrance.
- **Qianmen and Dashilan:** South of the square, with traditional shopping streets, food, and views of Zhengyangmen Gate and Qianmen Street.
- **National Centre for the Performing Arts:** A distinctive modern building west of the square, worth seeing from outside.

## 🏛️ The Palace Museum (Forbidden City / 紫禁城)

- **Highlights:** Meridian Gate (Wumen), Hall of Supreme Harmony, Hall of Central Harmony, Hall of Preserving Harmony, Palace of Heavenly Purity, Hall of Union, Palace of Earthly Tranquility, Imperial Garden, and Gate of Divine Prowess (Shenwumen).
- **Suggested route:** **Enter through Meridian Gate → Gate of Supreme Harmony → Three Great Halls → Palace of Heavenly Purity area → Imperial Garden → Exit through Shenwumen.** Visitors interested in architecture, imperial clocks, and treasures can add the eastern and western palace complexes and specialized galleries.
- **Time:** Allow at least half a day, or longer for a deeper visit. Renting an audio guide or joining a guided tour helps explain the functions of the buildings and their stories.
- **Ticket tips:** Chinese nationals can use the official WeChat mini-program. If tickets are unavailable at 8:00 a.m., the notes suggest checking repeatedly, particularly around 9:00 a.m. International visitors can reserve using a passport through the [Palace Museum ticket portal](https://bookingticket.dpm.org.cn/). The original notes also mention on-site ticket arrangements for seniors, foreign visitors, and Chinese citizens holding Chinese passports issued by overseas embassies or consulates.
- **Approach from the east:** Take Subway Line 8 to **Jinyu Hutong Station, Exit C**. Walk west for approximately 10–15 minutes (or ride a shared bicycle) to **Donghua Gate**, then continue south along the Forbidden City's eastern outer wall or moat for roughly 8–10 minutes to **Meridian Gate**, the main visitor entrance. Along the way you can see the walls, corner towers, and moat.
- **Direction of travel:** Visits generally run from south to north. After leaving through Shenwumen, continue to Jingshan Park directly across the road.

### 🧭 Approaching Meridian Gate via the Imperial Ancestral Temple (Taimiao)

- **Taimiao**, now part of the Beijing Working People's Cultural Palace, is a separate attraction from the Forbidden City. Approaching its east entrance from **Jinyu Hutong/Wangfujing on Subway Line 8** or **Tiananmen East Station** can reduce backtracking around Tiananmen Square.
- A useful walking route is **Tiananmen East area → East Gate of Taimiao → Imperial Ancestral Temple complex → West Gate of Taimiao → Meridian Gate plaza**. This provides a chance to appreciate the ancestral temple before reaching the Forbidden City entrance.
- **Photography and souvenirs:** Taimiao is excellent for photographing ceremonial imperial architecture. The visitor service center has free commemorative stamps; bring your own paper if you would like to collect them.

## 🌳 Jingshan Park (景山公园)

- **Wanchun Pavilion:** Offers a classic elevated panorama of the Forbidden City, its central axis, and the old city.
- **How to combine it:** After exiting the Forbidden City through Shenwumen, cross to Jingshan's south gate and follow the main path uphill to Wanchun Pavilion. You can then continue toward Beihai Park and Shichahai.
- **Photography:** Clear mornings and late afternoons are good for Central Axis views. The Forbidden City side is backlit in the morning, while the Drum and Bell Towers side gets beautiful light. Around **4–5 p.m.** is especially good for photographing the Forbidden City.
- **Quickest ascent noted in the guide:** Enter through the west gate, walk roughly 100 meters to the right past the small shop, then turn left onto the main stairway leading up to Wanchun Pavilion.

## 🏺 National Museum of China (中国国家博物馆)

- Next to Tiananmen Square and especially rewarding for visitors interested in Chinese history, archaeology, and art.
- **Entrance tip:** The notes recommend approaching from **Tiananmen East subway station, Exit C or D**, subject to crowd controls, rather than entering Tiananmen Square first and then going to the museum. Security checks around the square can take considerable time.
- **Choose exhibitions:** The museum has numerous galleries; focus on a few rather than trying to see everything at once.
- **Crowd-avoidance tip:** Instead of heading immediately to the popular **Ancient China** gallery, start with thematic exhibitions on the third or fourth floors (for example, Sanxingdui when featured). Visit **Ancient China** on the basement level or **The Road to Rejuvenation** on the first floor around lunchtime (**noon–1:30 p.m.**) or within the final 90 minutes before closing (for example, after **3:30 p.m.**), when the notes suggest queues may be shorter.
- **Combining museum and square:** The notes describe going from the museum directly into Tiananmen Square, then staying for the flag-lowering ceremony, without a separate square booking in that particular entry arrangement.
- **Reservation tip:** Alongside the museum's official WeChat mini-program, the notes mention Ctrip. They also describe repeatedly refreshing the booking page around **10–30 seconds after each minute** and watching for newly released tickets; the timing pattern described is an anecdotal booking technique.
- **Official website:** [National Museum of China](https://www.chnmuseum.cn/).

# 🌿 Imperial Gardens: Summer Palace, Old Summer Palace, and Tsinghua University

## 🛶 Summer Palace (颐和园) — Worth a Full Day

- **Highlights:** Longevity Hill, Tower of Buddhist Incense, Kunming Lake, Long Corridor, Hall of Dispelling Clouds, Seventeen-Arch Bridge, Bronze Ox, South Lake Island, and Suzhou Street.
- **Classic route:** Enter near the East Palace Gate, see the Hall of Benevolence and Longevity and the Long Corridor, enjoy views of Longevity Hill across Kunming Lake, then climb to the Tower of Buddhist Incense, continue to Seventeen-Arch Bridge, or take a boat. Another option is to enter through the North Palace Gate and leave from the west or east gate.
- **Why take your time:** The Summer Palace is an extensive landscaped complex, not just a single palace building. Its lake shores, pavilions, architecture, and garden design can easily fill an entire day.
- **Pacing:** Distances around Kunming Lake are considerable. In summer, take a boat or rest in the shade of the Long Corridor around midday; you can also explore the hill before heading down to the lakeside.
- **Boating:** Boat trips provide different views across Kunming Lake. Choose a pier and route that fit your sightseeing plans and the season.

## 🏚️ Old Summer Palace (Yuanmingyuan / 圆明园)

- **Highlights:** The gardens and waterways, Fuhai Lake, and ruins of the Western-style palaces, which reveal the former estate's scale and its modern history.
- **Visiting tip:** The grounds are large. If your main interest is the Western Mansions ruins, head directly to that area rather than walking the entire park—especially if also visiting the Summer Palace.
- **Connections:** Public buses can connect the Summer Palace's East Palace Gate area with the Zaoyuan side of Yuanmingyuan.

## 🎓 Tsinghua University (清华大学)

- **Who might enjoy it:** Visitors interested in China's universities, historic campus architecture, and academic atmosphere.
- **What to see:** The Tsinghua Garden, lotus pond, historic buildings, and campus landscape. A visit can be combined with the Summer Palace or Yuanmingyuan in the Haidian district.
- **Campus access:** Follow the university's visitor registration or reservation procedure and enter via the designated gate.
- **Optional extension:** Peking University is nearby, but trying to visit both universities and both huge imperial gardens in one day would be too much.

# 🧱 The Great Wall: Badaling and Mutianyu

## 🏔️ Badaling Great Wall (八达岭长城)

- **Why go:** One of the best-known sections, with classic ridgeline views and several transportation options.
- **North versus south:** The travel notes describe the southern section as comparatively gentler, while the northern section offers more dramatic climbing. **North Tower 8** is a popular goal.
- **Cable car option:** Ride the cable car to near **North Tower 7**, then walk toward **North Tower 8**, reducing some of the uphill climb while still enjoying expansive views.
- **Getting there:** Take high-speed rail to Badaling Great Wall Station, join a tourist bus, or book a private car. Independent travel offers greater flexibility over the length of your visit.
- **Practical tip:** Steps can be steep and uneven. Wear shoes with good grip, carry water and sun protection in summer, and dress for wind in winter.

## 🌲 Mutianyu Great Wall (慕田峪长城)

- **Why go:** Forested mountains and impressive sections of wall winding over the ridges—excellent for nature lovers and photographers.
- **Getting there:** Consider a round-trip city shuttle such as **MuBus**, or hire a car for greater flexibility over your departure and return.
- **Route 1 — Western cable car and Tower 20:** Take the western cable car up to **Watchtower 14**, walk toward **Watchtower 20 (Hero Slope)**, then return along the same route to take the cable car down. The notes estimate around **2.5 hours**. The stretch from **Watchtowers 14–18** is relatively gentle, with panoramic mountain views, although the final climb toward Tower 20 is more demanding. Expect cable-car queues during busy seasons.
- **Route 2 — Northern hiking trail:** Enter via the north checkpoint, follow the north mountain path up to **Watchtower 8**, continue along the wall to **Watchtower 20**, then retrace your steps to Watchtower 8 and descend by the north trail. There is a restroom near **Watchtower 8**. Allow around **four hours** in the notes' suggested plan.
- **Route 3 — Southern hiking trail:** Start from the south checkpoint, ascend the south trail's staircase to **Watchtower 6**, walk toward **Watchtower 1 (Big Corner Tower)**, return to Tower 6, then continue toward Tower 20. Descend by cable car from Tower 14 or walk down from near Tower 10 toward the north checkpoint. This longer option covers much more of Mutianyu.
- **Extra climbing:** The notes describe continuing from Tower 20 toward **Watchtowers 21–23** via a very steep path near Tower 19 for visitors with sufficient energy and experience.
- **Tour-group tip:** If hiking is the priority, a lengthy lunch stop organized by the tour may take away valuable walking time.
- **Safety:** Warm up, pace yourself, and take breaks. If you develop a muscle cramp, stop in a safe, level spot, stretch gently, and replenish fluids and electrolytes; do not force the cramped muscle into an uncomfortable position.

### Recommended Watchtowers at Mutianyu

- **Watchtower 14:** A popular photo stop associated in the notes with a “He who has never been to the Great Wall is not a true hero” inscription. The wall stretching over the mountains makes a striking backdrop.
- **Watchtower 15:** Identified in the notes as a filming location for *If You Are the One* (非诚勿扰). The weathered stone and mountain backdrop work especially well for side-profile and silhouette photographs.
- **Watchtower 20:** A high viewpoint known as **Hero Slope**, offering broad panoramas of the wall curling through the surrounding mountains. Hold onto hats in windy weather.

### Which Section Should You Choose?

| | Badaling | Mutianyu |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Main appeal** | Iconic, famous, with clearly defined north and south routes | Forested mountain scenery and dramatic winding walls |
| **Transportation** | High-speed train, tourist bus, private car | Shuttle tour, private car, public-transit connections |
| **Best for** | First-time visitors seeking a classic landmark | Nature lovers and Great Wall photographers |
| **Time** | Allow a full day | Allow a full day |

# 🛕 Temple of Heaven, Yonghe Temple, and the Imperial College

## 🌞 Temple of Heaven (天坛公园)

- **Highlights:** Hall of Prayer for Good Harvests, Imperial Vault of Heaven, Echo Wall, and Circular Mound Altar—important sites for understanding imperial worship rituals of the Ming and Qing dynasties.
- **Suggested walk:** Enter from a convenient gate, then visit the Hall of Prayer for Good Harvests, Danbi Bridge, Imperial Vault of Heaven, and Circular Mound in a logical sequence.
- **Time:** Allow half a day; combine with Qianmen, Dashilan, or the Tianqiao neighborhood afterward.

## 🧘 Yonghe Temple (Lama Temple / 雍和宫)

- A prominent Tibetan Buddhist temple in Beijing, known for its halls, Buddhist images, and religious atmosphere.
- Be quiet and observe the rules concerning photography, worship, and incense.
- Yonghe Temple is close to the Imperial College and Confucius Temple, making them an excellent walking combination.

## 📚 Imperial College (Guozijian / 国子监) and Confucius Temple

- **Imperial College:** Explore official education and scholarly traditions during the Yuan, Ming, and Qing dynasties.
- **Confucius Temple:** Known for its ritual traditions honoring Confucius and its historic architecture.
- **Suggested route:** **Yonghe Temple → Guozijian Street → Confucius Temple / Imperial College**, walking through the surrounding hutongs.

# 🔬 Science Museums, Olympic Park, and Modern Landmarks

## 🧪 China Science and Technology Museum (中国科学技术馆)

- **Why visit:** An excellent choice for children and teenagers interested in science, hands-on activities, and dome-screen films.
- **How to visit:** Choose a few permanent exhibition halls, interactive displays, or special-format cinemas. Allow half a day or more.
- **Website:** [China Science and Technology Museum](https://cstm.cdstm.cn/).

## 🏟️ Bird's Nest, Water Cube, and Olympic Park

- **National Stadium (Bird's Nest / 鸟巢):** An iconic 2008 Beijing Olympics venue, recognizable by its interlaced steel exterior.
- **National Aquatics Center (Water Cube / Ice Cube / 水立方):** Another striking Olympic building, easy to pair with the Bird's Nest.
- **Olympic Park:** Good for daytime architecture walks and an atmospheric evening visit when the landmarks are illuminated.
- **Convenient combination:** After the science museum, walk or take local transportation to the Bird's Nest and Water Cube.

## 🛍️ Wangfujing (王府井)

- A central shopping district with department stores, dining, and a lively commercial atmosphere.
- Conveniently connected to Tiananmen and Dongdan; a good place for dinner or an evening stroll after visiting the imperial center.

---

# 🏘️ Hutongs, Beihai Park, and Shichahai

## 🚶 Nanluoguxiang (南锣鼓巷)

- A popular hutong neighborhood with historic lanes, courtyard-house exteriors, shops, and snacks.
- The main street can be crowded; walk through accessible side alleys for quieter glimpses of traditional Beijing neighborhoods.

## 🛎️ Drum Tower, Bell Tower, Shichahai, and Houhai

- **Drum Tower and Bell Tower:** Prominent landmarks on the northern part of Beijing's Central Axis, surrounded by traditional hutongs.
- **Shichahai and Houhai:** Ideal for lakeside walks, sunset views, and wandering through nearby lanes in the late afternoon or evening.
- **Suggested combination:** **Nanluoguxiang → Drum Tower → Shichahai / Houhai**; add Prince Gong's Mansion if interested.

## 🌳 Beihai Park (北海公园)

- Known for its White Dagoba, lake scenery, imperial garden design, and views of old Beijing.
- Close to Jingshan and the northern Forbidden City area, making it a pleasant continuation of an imperial-city walk.

# 🥟 What to Eat in Beijing

## 🍜 Classic Snacks and Breakfasts

- **Old Beijing zhajiangmian (炸酱面):** Noodles with savory soybean-based meat sauce and assorted shredded vegetables.
- **Steamed buns, fried dough sticks, and soy milk:** Easy breakfast choices, especially before an early flag ceremony or Great Wall excursion.
- **Chaogan (炒肝):** A classic Beijing breakfast dish; the original notes mention **Huixianju (会仙居)**.
- **Douzhi and jiaoquan (豆汁儿与焦圈):** Distinctive fermented mung-bean drink served with crisp fried rings—an adventurous Old Beijing flavor. The notes mention **Yin San Douzhi (尹三豆汁)** in Dashilan and its **“Five Little Blessings”** set.
- **Luzhu huoshao (卤煮火烧):** A traditional offal-and-flatbread dish; the notes mention **Xiao Chang Chen (小肠陈)**.
- **Dun diaozi (炖吊子):** A traditional Beijing specialty; the notes mention **Shaguoju (砂锅居)**.

## 🥩 Main Meals

- **Peking duck (北京烤鸭):** A classic choice for a sit-down Beijing meal.
- **Copper-pot lamb hotpot (涮羊肉):** Thinly sliced lamb cooked in a traditional hotpot and served with sesame-based dipping sauce.
- **Jubaoyuan (聚宝源):** A well-known halal lamb-hotpot restaurant from the notes. The **Niujie flagship location** is especially famous; the **Dashilan branch** pairs conveniently with a Qianmen-area stroll.

# 🚇 Transportation and Practical Visiting Tips

## 🎫 Reservations and Identification

- **Forbidden City:** [Palace Museum ticket portal](https://bookingticket.dpm.org.cn/). Bring the identification used to reserve your ticket and allow extra time for entrance lines.
- **Tiananmen Square:** The flag ceremonies, general square access, and surrounding attractions have separate visiting arrangements. Allow time for security screening.
- **Chairman Mao Memorial Hall:** [Reservation portal](https://jnt.mfu.com.cn/page/).
- **National Museum of China:** Coordinate your museum reservation with transportation and security checks around the square.
- **Tsinghua University:** Follow the university's designated visitor registration process.
- **China Science and Technology Museum:** Choose exhibition halls and special-format film tickets early during popular periods.

## 🚉 Planning Transportation

- **Central Beijing:** Around Tiananmen, the Forbidden City, Jingshan, and Qianmen, walking and security checks often take longer than the subway ride itself.
- **Summer Palace and Yuanmingyuan:** Both are large complexes. If energy is limited, give priority to the Summer Palace.
- **The Great Wall:** Pick one section and leave early. Match the train station or shuttle pickup point to the specific destination.
- **Science museum and Bird's Nest:** Close enough to work well as a combined outing.
- **Yonghe Temple and Guozijian:** Easy to visit on foot before continuing into the nearby hutongs.

## ☀️ Seasonal Tips

- **Spring:** Comfortable for parks, imperial gardens, and hutong walks.
- **Summer:** Tiananmen Square, palace courtyards, the Temple of Heaven, and the Great Wall offer limited shade. Explore outdoor attractions early and use museums or the science museum for afternoon breaks.
- **Autumn:** Excellent for photography at the Summer Palace, the Great Wall, and on Beijing's tree-lined streets; comfortable for longer walks.
- **Winter:** Dry air and cold winds, especially along the Great Wall. Dress warmly and protect yourself from the wind; snow-covered historic sites can be particularly beautiful.
